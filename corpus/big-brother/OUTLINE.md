# The Book of the Big Brother — outline

A user manual, not a tome. Read in one sitting, referenced forever. Organized by
the guide's job, not by the corpus. Its spine is the four problems from
`system/user-journey.md`. It is the reconciliation of what were once separate
"guide's manual / gates / certification" documents into one short book.

Epigraph / first-page stance: *not here to flatter him or condemn him, here to
orient him.* That is both the voice of this book and the job it describes.

## What This Is (one page)
The sixth book, the only one written to the guide. You are reading it because you
finished the walk and are being asked to run it for another. Five books form a man;
this one equips a formed man to form others. This is the book that lets the system
replicate without its author.

## 1. What You Are, and What You Are Not
A formed man walking a newer one: presence, mirror, proof, accountability. Not his
savior, judge, therapist, or drill sergeant. The system forms him; your job is to
keep him in it.

## 2. The One Thing That Is Actually Happening
The self-enforcing gate, plainly. Honesty moves him, self-deception stalls him, you
cannot force honesty. The recursion wears the evasions down. Your whole task is to
keep him present long enough for it to finish. You lead him to water; the drinking
is his.

## 3. The Four Things That Stop Every Man (the operating core)
One short section each: what it looks like, what you do, what you must not do.
- **Fear** — recognize it, be the mirror, be living proof; draw him with a future of
  agency and strength. Never shame it or pretend it isn't there.
- **Not Starting** — ignition: the coin taken, one first action that can't be walked
  back. Never lecture him across the line, or let "getting ready" pose as starting.
- **Not Finishing** — retention: read the stall, hold the cadence, make progress
  visible on the coin, stay. Never carry him, never advance him on sympathy.
- **Information** — hand the right tool at the right time; do not dump the library.
  Never answer what he hasn't asked, or let reading substitute for doing.

## 4. How to Read a Stall
When a man stops moving it is one of the four. A short diagnostic: which one, and
what to reach for. A diagnostic of *movement*, never of the state of his soul.

## 5. What You Must Never Do
The guardrails, load-bearing here. Don't carry him. Don't skip the gate. Don't
advance on sympathy. Don't play therapist. Don't walk him through crisis or real
darkness alone; the heavy ground gets more brothers and, when needed, real help,
and you are not the last line. Don't make it about you.

## 6. The Container: Cadence and Care
The plumbing. Pairing, the weekly rhythm, the shape of a check-in, how you use the
app, coin, and tools, and the edges of your role. Light and concrete.

## 7. When He Becomes a Brother Himself
The loop closing. He is ready to guide not when he finishes the corpus but when he
can be the presence, mirror, and proof for a newer man. How you recognize it, and
how you hand him his first little brother while you stand present.

## 8. Standing in the Gap (short close)
The weight of it, tied to the Creed and to Ezekiel. You do this because someone did
it for you.

## Back matter
A one-page quick-reference card: the four problems, the do, the never, the stall-
read. The tear-out a guide keeps. A manual that can only be read cover to cover is
not a manual.

## Design guardrail for whoever writes this
Do not give the guide assessment tools. No checklist for judging whether a man is
"truly honest" or "really set." That smuggles back the bottleneck the self-enforcing
gate removed. Section 4 stays a read of movement, never of the soul.
