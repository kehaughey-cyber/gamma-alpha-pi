# Getting to Know Your Community

A worksheet for the Community aspects of the walk: know the people, history, and resources
around you well enough to connect and contribute. It moves a man from abstract "community"
to a specific place he can name, observe, and act in.

- **Source:** `Fraternity/Books/Getting to Know Your Community.docx`.
- **Where it fits:** the Love → Community and Vision → Community aspects of the
  self-assessment (`self-assessment.md`). Blank template; a man's answers are his own.
- **Not included here:** the source file also bundles an event-planning guide, a safety-plan
  template, and a **liability waiver** (real legal language). Those are operational/legal
  tooling, not a personal formation instrument; they belong in `operations/` (event runbook)
  and `governance/` (the waiver), and stay in the quarry until that lane is worked.

## Step 1 — Define your community
What does "community" mean to you: your neighborhood or city, a group you belong to (work,
church, club), people who share your values? Name the specific one you want to learn about.
- **My community is:** ___

## Step 2 — Observe
Spend time watching. Look for common gathering places (parks, centers, coffee shops), signs
of culture (murals, festivals, traditions), and challenges or needs.
- Where do people gather?
- What activities bring people together?
- What is the biggest challenge facing my community?

## Step 3 — Research
Learn the history, demographics, and organizations.
- **History:** the story of the place. Visit a library or historical society; talk to long-time residents.
- **Demographics:** who lives here. Census data, local statistics, the diversity of culture, age, profession.
- **Resources:** what exists. Nonprofits, government offices, clubs, schools, hospitals, parks.
- **Three interesting things I learned:** ___

## Step 4 — Engage
Take active steps to connect.
- **Attend an event** (a market, town hall, workshop).
- **Talk to people** — neighbors, local business owners; ask what they love about the place.
- **Volunteer** — a cleanup, food bank, mentoring program.

Reflection: What did I enjoy most about engaging? What surprised me?

## Step 5 — Analyze your role
- **What do I bring** — skills, resources, time I can offer?
- **What can I learn** — wisdom, experience, opportunities for growth?
- **What would I change or improve** — where can I make a positive impact?

Communities thrive when individuals take the time to care, connect, and act. This worksheet
is the first step; a service project (routed to the `operations/` event runbook) is the next.
