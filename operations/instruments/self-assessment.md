# The NTI Self-Assessment (the 45 aspects)

The core instrument of the walk: the Nosce Te Ipsum self-assessment that the Mine runs on
and the companion facilitates. A man rates himself across forty-five aspects of his life,
names where he wants to be, and reads the gap. This is the same forty-five that map to the
Living Coin (`companion/reference/living-coin.md`); this file is the fillable instrument,
with every aspect named.

- **Source:** `Fraternity/Books/GAP Self Assessment.xlsx` (the newer, clean generation; the
  older `Where I want to be.xlsx` is the same instrument with broken formulas — superseded).
- **Doctrine:** a man assesses himself. This is not graded. The companion and the big
  brother receive his numbers, reflect them, and watch whether the gap closes over time.
  Nothing here judges the state of his soul (`system/user-journey.md`). It is a mirror with
  a scale, not a scorecard someone else marks.

## How it works

For each of the forty-five aspects, a man answers on a 1–10 scale:

- **Where I am** — his honest current state. Each aspect has its own 1–10 ladder of what the
  levels look like in practice (for example, *Seek God First* runs from "1: minimal spiritual
  awareness" to "10: a profound, life-changing encounter with God"). The ladder keeps the
  number concrete instead of abstract.
- **Where I want to be** — his goal level. Not fantasy; the level he is actually moving toward.
- **Delta** — the gap between the two. A large delta flags where the work is.

Then he **prioritizes**: of the forty-five, which few matter most right now? A high delta on
a high-priority aspect is where the next sprint goes. The instrument is revisited across
seasons; the numbers are meant to change as the man does.

## Note on the source spreadsheet

The `.xlsx` carries the full 1–10 descriptive ladder for every aspect (hundreds of lines),
the delta math, per-tenet balances, and a priority-sorting view. Those descriptive ladders
are the one thing this Markdown does not yet reproduce; when a companion or a print workbook
needs them verbatim, pull them from the source sheet's "Self Assessment" tab. Never commit a
*filled-in* copy: a man's numbers are private to him (`companion/README.md`, job #2).
