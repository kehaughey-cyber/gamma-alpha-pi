# The Hill with the Ashes

Its primary purpose is to show the AI rewriting these books how Kevin thinks, how he moves from the concrete to the transcendent, how he carries guilt and grief without drowning in them, and how the most important realizations in his life arrived not through study or argument but through lived experience at its most extreme. # The Story

## Before the Fire

This story happens before the extension cord. Before the company that crossed the country building megachurches. Before Van Hughes and the financial crisis and the bankruptcy and the foreclosure. This is earlier. This is one of the many seasons of layoff that come with a life in construction, and in one of those long winters without work, I ended up drilling water and gas wells for a living.

The driller I worked for had 435 acres of property surrounded by state park in West Central Indiana. Beautiful land. Remote. About a half mile in from the end of a dead end road sat an old trailer that his father had lived in. He let me rent it. So for that season I was living alone on 435 acres of state park land, a half mile from the nearest neighbor, surrounded by nothing but field and tree line and sky.

We worked brutal hours. Four in the morning until eight or nine at night, seven days a week. The pay was modest by the hour, but the hours made it workable. I was not living alone in that trailer. I had my brother and two friends with me. We had pooled what we had left from my first company attempt, a steel erections business building pre-engineered metal buildings that I had started and lost on the morning of September 11th, 2001. I had two years of work lined up that morning. By nightfall I had nothing. That collapse is what had led me to the well driller in the first place.

What remained from that life, the tools, the gear, the pieces of what we had been trying to build, was stored in the barn on the property. Along with all of our music equipment. We were in a band. I sang. That barn held what was left of two different versions of a life I was still trying to figure out how to rebuild.

## The Fire

One February day, we got off work early. I do not even remember why. But it was still daylight when we got home, which was rare. I was working on the trailer, and part of that work meant burning off some trash. We used an old horse trough as a burn barrel.

What I did not know was that someone had put an aerosol can in the barrel.

I lit the fire. I watched it for a while, made sure it was safe, and went inside to get a drink. When I came back out, the can had already exploded. The fire had been blown across the field. The field had caught. The wind had picked up and pushed the fire into the barn. The barn was burning.

We did not go for our personal belongings. We went for the music equipment. That tells you something about who we were and what mattered to us. We started dragging instruments and gear out of a burning barn that was full of round bales and hay wagons and tractors. The smoke was getting thicker. The fire was building fast.

I looked around and I did not see my brother.

I thought he was inside.

I ran into the burning building to find him.

I got to the back of the shop area, where I believed he was, and I called out for him. He called back from outside. He was already out. Which meant I was in alone, in a building that was collapsing, with no way to see and no air to breathe.

I dropped to the ground looking for breathable air. My face against the cold concrete. There was nothing. The smoke was all the way down. I could not see. I could not breathe. I stood up and ran toward the sound of his voice, through what was left of our gear and what was left of my life, and I came out of that building as it came down around me. The walls were brushing against me as they fell.

I ran into a burning building for my brother. That is who I was. That is who I am. The building does not change that.

## The Morning After

My brother went to the hospital that night. The fire had boiled the skin on his arms. Third degree burns. I had done that. I had lit the fire. Whatever rationalization a man might reach for, that is the weight I was carrying the next morning when we went through the ashes.

Pearl Jam's Black was playing in my head. It would not stop. And now my bitter hands cradle broken glass of what was everything. I held ashes and those words held me. We went through what remained and found nothing worth salvaging. Everything was gone.

I sat down on the hill above the burn site looking out over the valley. It was a beautiful vista. The kind of view that does not care what you have just lost. I was feeling very sorry for myself, which was warranted. My brother was burned because of me. Everything we had stored in that barn was ash. I had nothing, again, and this time I had taken people I loved down with me.

## The Shoes

I started looking at my shoes.

I am a logical person. I try to understand things. And I could not understand what I was looking at. My shoes had melted. The nylon had warped and fused. I had been wearing basketball shorts and a t-shirt when I ran into that fire. I should have burns. I should have been hurt. My brother's arms had boiled inside that same building. But I came out unharmed. The only evidence that I had been in a building hot enough to collapse was on the soles of my feet.

My shoes had melted and I was not burned.

I sat with that fact on that hill. My logical mind turned it over and could not make it fit any framework I had. And in that gap between what should have happened and what did, something shifted.

I realized God was real.

Not as an argument. Not as a conclusion reached through study or debate. As a fact that arrived in the space where logic ran out. And shortly after that realization, I felt something I can only describe as a presence. Someone standing over me. I went to look. It felt like a hand on my shoulder. And in that instant, I saw my entire life before that moment. Everything I had done. Everything I had been. The decisions and the failures and the things I am not proud of. All of it.

I believe it was God putting his hand on me and showing me that I am not my things. I am not what I have accomplished. I am something far more.

## What It Was the Beginning Of

I still had grief to process. I still had guilt to carry for a while. Losing the barn, my brother's burns, the collapse of that particular season of trying. None of it resolved cleanly or quickly. Emotional truth does not work that way.

But that hill was the beginning of my walk with God. And it was the beginning of an understanding that has shaped everything I have built since, including Gamma Alpha Pi and every book in this collection.

I am not my things. I am not my accomplishments. I am not my failures or my losses or the businesses that collapsed or the homes I lost or the crews I could not hold together. I am something that exists prior to and independent of all of that.

So are you.

That is not a motivational statement. It is a fact that arrived through fire, through my brother's burned arms, through melted shoes on a February hillside in West Central Indiana. You do not have to earn it. You do not have to understand it yet. But you are going to need to know it.

WHAT THIS STORY TEACHES ABOUT VOICE

Notice the sequence. The story earns every transcendent moment through specific, grounded, physical detail before it arrives there. Four AM start times. 435 acres. A horse trough used as a burn barrel. An aerosol can. Cold concrete against a face with no air to breathe. The melted soles of basketball shoes.

The physical detail is not decoration. It is the foundation. The reader has to be able to smell the smoke and feel the cold concrete before the hill means anything. The moment of transcendence lands because everything before it was earned through the specific and the real.

Notice also how the guilt is held. It is not deflected. It is not explained away. 'I had done that. I had lit the fire.' The voice owns what is its to own and then keeps moving. It does not wallow. It does not perform remorse. It states the weight and carries it forward. That is what an honest man sounds like.

Notice the logic. Even in the most spiritually significant moment of the story, the voice comes through a logical mind trying to make sense of something that defies logic. 'My shoes had melted and I was not burned.' The faith does not arrive by abandoning reason. It arrives in the place where reason runs out. That is the honest shape of how this man thinks and how this voice moves.

Write toward that. Ground everything in the specific. Earn the transcendent. Own the weight without drowning in it. And trust that truth, stated plainly and without decoration, is its own form of power.
