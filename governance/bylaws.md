# Preamble

These Bylaws are the operational expression of the Institutional Constitution of Gamma Alpha Pi. Where the Constitution establishes what GAP is, these Bylaws govern how it functions. Where the Constitution sets the soul of the institution, these Bylaws set its bones.

These Bylaws are subordinate to the Constitution in all things. Any provision of these Bylaws that conflicts with the Constitution is void. These Bylaws may be amended by the Leadership Council through the process established herein, but no amendment may contradict, circumvent, or diminish any provision of the Constitution.

These Bylaws are written with intention on one critical matter: they do not unnecessarily constrain the institution or its leaders. Gamma Alpha Pi is built to grow. It is built to own property, to run events, to fill stadiums, to extend its resources generously into the world, to employ talent, to create content, to enter partnerships, and to do all things that a healthy, mission-driven institution does at scale. These Bylaws provide the governance framework within which that growth occurs cleanly, legally, and in alignment with the mission. They are a structure for freedom, not a cage.

## ARTICLE I — Organizational Structure

### §1.1  The Three-Entity Architecture

Gamma Alpha Pi operates through three legally distinct entities as established in the Constitution. Each entity has its own governance documents, its own tax status, and its own operational scope. These Bylaws govern the Fraternal Brotherhood entity specifically. Where applicable, provisions reference the governance relationship between all three entities.

The three entities are: Gamma Alpha Pi Fraternal Brotherhood, a domestic fraternal society organized under Internal Revenue Code Section 501(c)(10), which governs the brotherhood, the chapters, the initiation process, the curriculum, and all member-exclusive programs and benefits; the Gamma Alpha Pi Foundation, a public charity organized under Internal Revenue Code Section 501(c)(3), which governs public-facing programs, community benefit activities, the 3-Day Reset as a public resource, educational content, charitable grants, and endowment; and the Gamma Alpha Pi Intellectual Property Company, a for-profit entity organized as an S-Corporation or LLC, which owns all intellectual property of the institution and licenses it to both nonprofit entities at arm's-length fair market rates.

### §1.2  Institutional Capacity

Gamma Alpha Pi in all its entities is authorized and empowered to: own and operate real property including retreat centers, chapter houses, event venues, and administrative facilities; enter contracts, leases, and partnership agreements; employ staff and engage independent contractors; produce, distribute, and monetize content across all media platforms; organize and operate events of any scale, including large-scale public gatherings, conferences, retreats, and community programs; accept gifts, grants, donations, bequests, and endowment contributions; make grants to other organizations whose missions align with GAP's mission; borrow money and issue debt instruments as needed for mission-aligned purposes; and do all other things that a lawfully organized institution may do in furtherance of its mission.

No enumeration of institutional powers in these Bylaws shall be construed to limit the institution to only those powers enumerated. GAP has the full legal capacity of its organizational form and these Bylaws govern how that capacity is exercised, not whether it exists.

### §1.3  The IP Relationship

The Intellectual Property Company owns all marks, curriculum materials, book titles, coin designs, digital assets, software, and any other creative or proprietary work product developed in connection with Gamma Alpha Pi. Both nonprofit entities operate under license from the IP Company. License fees are set at arm's-length fair market rates, documented in formal license agreements, reviewed annually, and approved by the Compensation Committee to ensure they do not constitute improper self-dealing.

The IP structure protects the institution's most valuable assets from any governance dispute, leadership transition, or attempted hostile acquisition. The assets of the brotherhood and the foundation can be governed and transferred only through the processes established in this document and the Constitution. The intellectual property that makes those assets valuable is held separately and is not subject to the same governance proceedings.

## ARTICLE II — The Leadership Council

### §2.1  Composition

The Leadership Council consists of nine voting members. One of the nine serves as Chairman. The Founder holds a permanent advisory seat with the rights and limitations established in the Constitution. All nine voting members hold equal voting authority on all substantive matters.

### §2.2  Qualifications

To serve on the Leadership Council, an individual must be a member of Gamma Alpha Pi in good standing, must have completed the core curriculum, must have served as a Big Brother, must affirm the GAP Creed and the Stewardship Covenant, and must not have any conflict of interest that would impair their ability to govern the institution in the interest of its members and mission. The council determines qualifications for any individual seat by majority vote.

### §2.3  Terms

Council members serve staggered three-year terms. To establish staggered terms at founding, three members are designated to initial one-year terms, three to two-year terms, and three to three-year terms, determined by lot at the founding council meeting. Thereafter all terms are three years. A council member may serve no more than three consecutive three-year terms before rotating off for a minimum of one full term. A former council member who has rotated off is eligible for re-election after sitting out one full term.

### §2.4  The Chairman

The Chairman is elected by the council from among its nine members by a majority vote. The Chairman serves a two-year term in the chair and may serve no more than two consecutive terms as Chairman, regardless of their remaining term as a council member. The Chairman convenes all council meetings, represents the institution publicly in a governance capacity, serves as the primary liaison between the council and chapter leadership, and holds procedural authority in council proceedings. The Chairman votes on all substantive matters as one of nine equal votes.

### §2.5  Election of Council Members

Vacancies on the Leadership Council are filled by a vote of the remaining council members. When a seat becomes vacant, the council identifies and evaluates candidates through a process it establishes, conducts a majority vote, and seats the new member for the remainder of the vacated term. If more than three seats become vacant simultaneously, the council convenes an extraordinary session within thirty days to fill all vacancies before conducting any other institutional business.

### §2.6  Resignation and Removal

A council member may resign at any time by written notice to the Chairman. Resignation is effective upon receipt unless a later date is specified. Removal requires a supermajority of seven of the nine votes as established in the Constitution. A member under review for removal does not vote on their removal. Removal proceedings are initiated by written request of any three council members and must be conducted within sixty days of the written request.

### §2.7  Meetings

The council meets no fewer than four times per year in regular session. Meetings may be held in person, by video conference, or by a combination of both. The Chairman calls all regular meetings with no fewer than fourteen days notice. Special meetings may be called by the Chairman or by written request of any four council members, with no fewer than seventy-two hours notice except in an emergency as declared by the Chairman.

Meetings are conducted according to Robert's Rules of Order except where these Bylaws or the Constitution provide otherwise.

### §2.8  Quorum

A quorum for the transaction of business is six of the nine voting members. No substantive vote may be taken without a quorum present. Procedural matters may be handled without a quorum at the Chairman's discretion but are subject to ratification at the next meeting at which a quorum is present.

### §2.9  Voting Thresholds

Simple majority (five of nine) governs operational decisions including budget approval, program adoption, staff hiring, event authorization, property transactions up to a threshold established annually by the council, and all other ordinary business. Supermajority (seven of nine) governs constitutional amendments, council member removal, the Chairman election, significant property acquisitions above the annually established threshold, any external partnership that commits the institution for more than three years, and any decision that materially alters the mission scope of the institution. Unanimous vote (nine of nine) governs changes to the Sacred Commitments of the Constitution and the Non-Acquisition Commitment, consistent with the Constitution.

### §2.10  Council Subcommittees

The Leadership Council may establish standing or ad hoc subcommittees to carry out specific governance functions. The following standing subcommittees are established by these Bylaws: the Compensation Committee, the Audit and Finance Committee, the Chapter Development Committee, and the Mission Integrity Committee. Each subcommittee is governed by a charter adopted by the council. Subcommittee recommendations are advisory to the council unless the council has delegated specific decision-making authority by majority vote.

## ARTICLE III — The Compensation Committee

The Compensation Committee is the most critical governance safeguard in these Bylaws. It exists to ensure that every dollar paid to the Founder, to council members, to executives, and to any person with significant influence over the institution is set by a process that is independent, documented, benchmarked, and legally defensible. It protects the institution from IRS scrutiny. It protects the Founder from accusations of self-dealing. And it protects the members from the misuse of institutional resources by those entrusted to steward them.

### §3.1  Composition

The Compensation Committee consists of three members of the Leadership Council, none of whom may be the Founder, a family member of the Founder, or any individual whose compensation is being reviewed. Members are appointed by council majority vote and serve two-year terms on the committee. The committee elects its own chair from among its three members.

### §3.2  Authority

The Compensation Committee has sole authority to set, review, and approve the compensation of the following individuals: the Founder, in any capacity in which the Founder receives compensation from any GAP entity; the Chairman of the Leadership Council; any executive director, chief operating officer, or equivalent senior staff position; and any council member who receives compensation from the institution beyond reimbursement of expenses.

No compensation for any of the above individuals may be paid without prior written approval by the Compensation Committee. Compensation set by the committee is binding unless overturned by a supermajority of seven council votes, excluding any interested parties.

### §3.3  The Compensation Standard

All compensation approved by the Compensation Committee must meet the Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonableness standard established by the IRS under Section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code. This requires that compensation be approved by an authorized body composed entirely of individuals who do not have a conflict of interest, that the approving body obtain and rely upon appropriate comparability data before making its determination, and that the approving body adequately document the basis for its determination contemporaneously with the decision.

Comparability data must include compensation paid by at least three organizations of similar size, mission, and geographic scope for comparable roles. The committee documents its comparability sources, its deliberation, and its rationale in writing before any compensation decision takes effect.

### §3.4  Founder Compensation Framework

The Founder may receive compensation from GAP entities through the following mechanisms, each governed separately by the Compensation Committee:

Executive compensation for services rendered in an operational role, set annually by the committee based on comparability data and the scope of the Founder's actual responsibilities to the institution. This compensation is paid by the entity for which the services are rendered and is reported on the appropriate tax filings.

Speaking and appearance fees for events organized by or on behalf of GAP entities, set at fair market rates benchmarked against comparable speakers and event organizers. The Founder may also receive compensation for speaking and appearances at events not organized by GAP without restriction, as this is personal professional income.

IP licensing income flowing from the IP Holding Company to the Founder as an owner of that entity is not compensation from the nonprofit entities and is not governed by the Compensation Committee. It is governed by the operating agreement of the IP Company and applicable tax law. The committee reviews the license fee structure annually to confirm it is at arm's-length fair market rates and does not constitute constructive distribution from the nonprofit entities.

### §3.5  Annual Review

The Compensation Committee conducts a formal review of all covered compensation no less than annually, prior to the adoption of the annual budget. The review assesses whether current compensation remains within the range of comparability data, whether the scope of services has changed materially, and whether any new compensation arrangements have been entered into since the last review.

The results of the annual review are documented in writing, presented to the full Leadership Council, and preserved in the permanent institutional record. The council does not vote on compensation decisions but receives the committee's report and may raise concerns through the Mission Integrity Committee if any member believes the committee's determination may violate the institution's mission or legal obligations.

### §3.6  Conflict of Interest Policy

Any council member, committee member, staff member, or volunteer who has a personal financial interest in a compensation decision must disclose that interest in writing and must recuse themselves from all deliberation and voting on that decision. Recusal is self-executing upon disclosure. The failure to disclose a conflict of interest is grounds for removal from the council or termination of employment or engagement.

## ARTICLE IV — Chapters

### §4.1  The Chapter as Primary Unit

The chapter is where the mission lives. Every program, every curriculum, every rite of passage, and every relationship of the brotherhood finds its expression in the chapter. The Leadership Council governs the institution. The chapters serve the brothers.

### §4.2  Chapter Charter

No group of men may operate as a chapter of Gamma Alpha Pi without a formally ratified chapter charter issued by the Leadership Council. The charter application process requires a minimum founding membership of seven brothers, a designated chapter leader who has completed the full curriculum and served as a Big Brother, a defined geographic service area, a chapter governance plan consistent with these Bylaws, and a financial plan demonstrating the chapter's path to self-sufficiency.

The Leadership Council votes on charter applications by simple majority. A ratified charter grants the chapter the right to use the GAP name and marks under license from the IP Company, to conduct chapter programs under the authority of the institution, and to represent GAP in its defined geographic service area.

### §4.3  Chapter Leadership

Each chapter is led by a Chapter Leader elected by the chapter's brothers in good standing by majority vote. The Chapter Leader serves a two-year term and may serve no more than three consecutive terms. The Chapter Leader is responsible for the chapter's operational health, its compliance with the Constitution and Bylaws, its financial management, and its relationship with the Leadership Council.

Each chapter also designates a Chapter Treasurer and a Chapter Secretary. The roles and responsibilities of these positions are established in the Chapter Operations Manual, which is adopted by the Leadership Council and updated as needed.

### §4.4  Chapter Meetings

Each chapter meets no fewer than twice per month in regular session. Chapter meetings are the primary arena of brotherhood, accountability, curriculum engagement, and community planning. Attendance is an expectation of membership, not an option. The Chapter Operations Manual establishes the standard for meeting conduct and the process for managing persistent non-attendance.

### §4.5  Chapter Finances

Each chapter maintains its own financial accounts, collects dues from its members, and manages its operating expenses. Chapter finances are governed by the financial policies established in Article VII of these Bylaws and by the Chapter Operations Manual. Chapters submit quarterly financial reports to the Leadership Council. The Audit and Finance Committee reviews chapter financial health as part of the institutional financial reporting cycle.

### §4.6  Chapter Discipline and Revocation

The Leadership Council may place a chapter on probationary status, suspend its charter, or revoke its charter for failure to comply with the Constitution or Bylaws, persistent financial mismanagement, conduct that brings harm or reputational damage to the institution, or failure to maintain the minimum membership and program standards established by the council.

Charter revocation requires a supermajority vote of seven council members following a minimum thirty-day notice period during which the chapter has the opportunity to respond. A chapter whose charter is revoked ceases all use of the GAP name, marks, and materials immediately upon revocation.

### §4.7  Chapter Property

Property acquired by a chapter in the name of Gamma Alpha Pi is institutional property, not chapter property. If a chapter's charter is revoked or the chapter dissolves, its assets are transferred to the institution for redistribution to other chapters or for mission-aligned use as determined by the Leadership Council.

## ARTICLE V — Membership Operations

### §5.1  Initiation Process

The initiation process is the gateway into full membership in Gamma Alpha Pi. It includes completion of the 3-Day Reset as the entry experience, a formal application and interview conducted by chapter leadership, assignment of a Big Brother for the duration of the Little Brother experience, completion of the prescribed curriculum within the time period established by the Chapter Operations Manual, participation in the rite of passage ceremony, and formal reception into the chapter by the brotherhood.

The initiation process is a human experience. No element of the initiation may be conducted exclusively through digital means. The rite of passage is witnessed by brothers and received from brothers.

### §5.2  Dues

Each entity of the institution may establish dues or fees appropriate to its programs and members. Membership dues for the Fraternal Brotherhood are set annually by the Leadership Council on the recommendation of the Audit and Finance Committee. Dues must be sufficient to support chapter operations and institutional overhead but must not be set at levels that create a barrier to membership for men who are otherwise qualified and committed.

The institution maintains a scholarship or dues-assistance fund to ensure that financial hardship is never the reason a qualified man cannot become a brother. The fund is administered by the Chapter Development Committee under guidelines established by the Leadership Council.

### §5.3  Member Records

The institution maintains accurate records of all members, including membership status, initiation date, Big Brother assignment, curriculum progress, and chapter affiliation. Member records are institutional property and are governed by the data privacy policies established by the Leadership Council consistent with applicable law and the AI Governance Codex.

### §5.4  Separation Proceedings

When a member's conduct warrants consideration of separation from the brotherhood, the following process governs: the Chapter Leader initiates a written notice of concern to the member and to the Leadership Council; the member has fourteen days to respond in writing; the Chapter Leader and two council-appointed brothers meet with the member within thirty days of the initial notice; the Leadership Council reviews the matter and votes on the appropriate response, which may range from a formal warning to temporary suspension to permanent separation; separation from the institution requires a supermajority of seven council votes.

No member is separated without the opportunity to be heard. No separation decision is made by any automated system. All proceedings are documented and preserved in the institutional record, accessible only to the council and the chapter leader involved.

## ARTICLE VI — Institutional Activities and Property

### §6.1  Events and Programs

Gamma Alpha Pi is authorized and encouraged to organize events of any scale in furtherance of its mission. This includes but is not limited to chapter meetings and retreats, regional brotherhood gatherings, large-scale public events and conferences, rites of passage ceremonies, community service programs, educational seminars, media productions, and any other activity consistent with the mission.

The Leadership Council approves events above a budget threshold it sets annually. The Chairman approves events below that threshold. The Chapter Leader approves chapter-level events within the chapter's budget authority. No approval hierarchy is intended to discourage ambitious programming. It is intended to ensure that the council maintains visibility into major institutional activities and can support them appropriately.

### §6.2  Real Property

GAP may own, lease, operate, and dispose of real property in furtherance of its mission. This includes retreat centers, chapter houses, event venues, administrative offices, and any other property that serves the brotherhood or the public benefit mission. Property acquisitions above the annually established threshold require a supermajority council vote. Property may be held by any of the three institutional entities as appropriate to its use and tax treatment.

The institution is encouraged to build a property portfolio that supports its mission at scale. Retreat centers provide the physical space for the rites of passage, for deep brotherhood formation, and for the kind of transformative experience that cannot happen in a conference call. Owning the spaces where transformation happens is consistent with the long-term mission of the institution.

### §6.3  Content and Media

GAP produces content across all media platforms as a primary mechanism of mission expansion and member acquisition. Content production is authorized without specific council approval provided it is consistent with the mission and the brand standards established by the Leadership Council. The Founder and designated content leaders have standing authority to produce and distribute content on behalf of the institution.

Revenue generated by institutional content belongs to the entity that produced it, governed by the revenue allocation framework established by the Audit and Finance Committee. Content created by the Founder in a personal capacity, separate from his institutional role, is his personal property and generates personal income. The boundary between institutional and personal content is defined in the Founder's engagement agreement with the institution, reviewed annually by the Compensation Committee.

### §6.4  Partnerships and Licensing

GAP may enter partnerships with other organizations, license its programs to qualified partners, and engage in collaborative activities that extend the mission's reach. All partnerships that commit the institution for more than three years or that involve the GAP name or marks require council approval. Partnerships are evaluated against the mission alignment standard: do they bring more men into genuine brotherhood, or do they substitute for it?

The IP Company manages all licensing of GAP intellectual property to external parties. No external party may use the GAP name, marks, curriculum, or other proprietary materials without a formal license agreement issued by the IP Company.

### §6.5  Charitable Giving and Grants

The Gamma Alpha Pi Foundation is authorized to make grants to other organizations whose missions align with GAP's mission of serving men and communities. Grant-making is governed by a grants policy established by the Foundation's board. The institution is committed to being generous with its resources as it grows. Giving is an expression of the mission, not a deviation from it.

## ARTICLE VII — Financial Governance

### §7.1  Fiscal Year

The fiscal year of Gamma Alpha Pi runs from January 1 through December 31.

### §7.2  Annual Budget

The Leadership Council adopts an annual budget no later than thirty days before the start of each fiscal year. The budget is prepared by the executive leadership of the institution, reviewed by the Audit and Finance Committee, and presented to the full council for approval by simple majority. The budget includes projected revenue by stream, operating expenses by category, chapter allocations, reserve contributions, endowment contributions, and compensation for all covered positions.

The budget is the primary financial governance tool of the institution. Expenditures that exceed any budget line by more than fifteen percent require council notification. Expenditures that exceed any budget line by more than twenty-five percent require council approval.

### §7.3  The Audit and Finance Committee

The Audit and Finance Committee consists of three council members appointed by the council for two-year terms. The committee oversees the financial health of the institution, reviews monthly financial statements, commissions the annual independent audit, monitors reserve and runway levels, reviews chapter financial reports, and presents a quarterly financial summary to the full council.

The committee has authority to flag any financial matter it deems material for full council review. No financial matter flagged by the committee may be tabled for more than thirty days without a full council vote on how to proceed.

### §7.4  Independent Audit

The institution engages an independent certified public accountant to conduct an annual audit of its financial statements. The audit is commissioned by the Audit and Finance Committee, conducted by a firm with no other financial relationship to the institution or its leadership, and presented to the full Leadership Council. The audit report is preserved in the institutional record and, in summary form, made available to members upon request.

### §7.5  Reserve Policy

The institution maintains an operating reserve equal to no fewer than six months of total operating expenses, held in liquid form accessible within thirty days. The council reviews and may adjust this minimum annually. When the reserve falls below the minimum, the Audit and Finance Committee presents a reserve restoration plan to the council within sixty days. Reserve restoration is treated as an institutional priority until the minimum is restored.

### §7.6  The Reinvestment Standard

Consistent with the Financial Stewardship Intelligence framework and the Constitution, all institutional surplus is allocated to mission reinvestment, reserve building, or endowment. No surplus is distributed to any individual in any form not authorized by the Compensation Committee. The Audit and Finance Committee confirms compliance with this standard in each quarterly report to the council.

### §7.7  Expense Authority

The following expense authority levels apply across all institutional entities. Individual transactions up to five thousand dollars may be approved by a designated staff member or the Chapter Leader within their chapter. Transactions between five thousand and twenty-five thousand dollars require the Chairman's approval or the approval of a council member designated by the Chairman. Transactions between twenty-five thousand and one hundred thousand dollars require the Chairman's approval plus the concurrence of one additional council member. Transactions above one hundred thousand dollars require a council majority vote.

These thresholds do not apply to recurring budgeted expenses that have already received council approval through the annual budget process. They apply to new or unbudgeted expenditures.

### §7.8  Borrowing Authority

The institution may borrow money in furtherance of its mission. Borrowing of any amount requires council majority approval. Borrowing above five hundred thousand dollars requires a supermajority of seven council votes. The Audit and Finance Committee reviews all outstanding debt obligations quarterly and reports to the council.

## ARTICLE VIII — Mission Integrity Committee

### §8.1  Purpose

The Mission Integrity Committee exists to ensure that the institution never drifts from its constitutional mission without someone noticing and saying so. It is the internal conscience of the governance structure. It has no executive authority. It has the authority to be heard.

### §8.2  Composition

The Mission Integrity Committee consists of three council members appointed for two-year terms and two brothers-at-large elected by the broader membership for two-year terms. The inclusion of non-council members ensures that the committee's perspective is not limited to the governing body alone.

### §8.3  Authority and Process

Any member of the institution may bring a concern about mission integrity to the committee in writing. The committee reviews all concerns, may conduct its own inquiry, and presents its findings and recommendations to the full Leadership Council. The council must respond to any Mission Integrity Committee finding within sixty days. The committee's findings are preserved in the institutional record.

The committee has standing to review any institutional decision, program, partnership, financial arrangement, or governance action for mission alignment. It may not veto decisions but it may require the council to formally affirm that a decision is mission-consistent before that decision takes effect.

## ARTICLE IX — Succession and Institutional Continuity

### §9.1  Succession Planning

The Leadership Council maintains a living succession plan updated no less than annually. The succession plan identifies, for each council seat and each key operational role, at least two individuals who are prepared to assume that role with minimal transition time. Succession planning is treated as a primary governance obligation, not a contingency measure.

### §9.2  Founder Transition

The Constitution establishes the Founder's advisory role and the transition of the Founder's constitutional veto authority to the council upon the Founder's departure from active participation. The Founder's transition is governed by a Founder Transition Plan developed by the council in collaboration with the Founder, updated every three years, and designed to ensure that the institution's mission, culture, and operational continuity are fully preserved through and after the transition.

The Founder Transition Plan addresses the Founder's ongoing relationship with the institution after transition, including any advisory or ambassador role the Founder may continue to hold, the transition of any compensation arrangements, and the management of the Founder's personal brand relationship with the institution.

### §9.3  Institutional Memory

The institution maintains a permanent record of its governance decisions, its financial history, its membership records, its program outcomes, and its amendment history. This institutional memory is a primary asset of the organization and is managed with the same care as any financial asset. The records are digitally preserved, backed up in multiple locations, and accessible to the Leadership Council at all times.

## ARTICLE X — Amendment of Bylaws

### §10.1  Amendment Authority

These Bylaws may be amended by the Leadership Council by simple majority vote, provided that the proposed amendment has been submitted in writing to all council members no fewer than fourteen days before the vote, that no amendment contradicts any provision of the Constitution, and that the amendment is documented in the Bylaws Amendment Record with the date, the vote count, and the rationale.

### §10.2  Constitutional Supremacy

Any amendment to these Bylaws that is found to conflict with any provision of the Constitution is void upon adoption. The Mission Integrity Committee reviews all Bylaws amendments for constitutional consistency within thirty days of adoption and reports any conflicts to the council for immediate correction.

### §10.3  Bylaws Amendment Record

Every amendment to these Bylaws is recorded in a permanent amendment log including the original text, the amended text, the date of adoption, the vote count, and the names of council members voting. This record is maintained alongside the Constitution's amendment record as part of the permanent institutional governance archive.

# Closing Statement

These Bylaws are the operating system of Gamma Alpha Pi. They govern the mechanics of a brotherhood that is built to grow without limit in the service of men and communities, while remaining governed with integrity at every scale.

They are intentionally permissive about what the institution may do and intentionally precise about how it must be governed when it does those things. GAP may own stadiums. It may fill them. It may build retreat centers, produce content that reaches millions, extend its generosity to communities around the world, and compensate the people who make all of that possible at rates that are fair, transparent, and legally sound. None of that is in tension with the mission. All of it, done with integrity, is the mission made large.

What these Bylaws protect against is the corruption of success. The governance structures here exist because organizations that grow without governance discipline eventually become something other than what they were built to be. These structures ensure that GAP at scale is still GAP. That the men it serves at scale are still being genuinely transformed. That the money it generates at scale is still being stewarded for the mission. And that the leaders who govern it at scale are still accountable to the same standard they ask of every brother.

We build large so we can serve many. We govern carefully so the many are genuinely served.

Gamma Alpha Pi Leadership Council
